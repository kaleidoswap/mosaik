Mosaik

A covenant DEX on Liquid.
The order is the coin.
The problem

An order is a promise.

  • A centralized exchange holds your funds — and can fail.
  • A market maker quotes a price — and can renege.
  • Every venue between you and the trade is a party you trust.

What if the order simply could not break?

The idea

The order is the coin.

A maker locks asset A into one Liquid coin governed by a Simplicity covenant — a Tessera.

An order isn't a promise a market maker might break. It's a covenant the coin itself keeps.
The name · Roma antiqua

A token, and the picture
it builds.

tessera
A token in ancient Rome. The tessera hospitalis was a small tablet snapped in two — each party kept a half. Rejoin the halves and they fit: the bond is proven. A bearer token, redeemed by fit — no registry, no signature, no trusted third party.
musīvum
From opus musīvum — a picture laid from thousands of tesserae. No single tile is the image. Set side by side, the independent pieces become one.

Every Mosaik offer is one Tessera — a covenant coin redeemed when the matching transaction fits. The order book is the mosaic they form.

The Tessera

Two doors. No keys.

SETTLE
Spendable by anyone — if the transaction pays the maker exactly amount_b of asset_b. One atomic swap. The coin refuses any spend that underpays.
REFUND · keyless
After timeout, anyone may sweep the coin — but only back to the maker. No key, no signature. The maker can stay offline forever.
How a swap runs

One transaction. Both legs, or neither.

MAKER
Locks asset A in a Tessera. Goes offline.
TAKER
Finds it on the Nostr orderbook.
TAKER
Builds one tx — pays the maker, takes asset A.
COVENANT
The node runs it — confirms, or rejects.

No escrow. No matching engine. No maker server.

Why Simplicity

A coin that reads its own
spending transaction.

Bitcoin Script cannot inspect the outputs of the transaction spending a coin. Simplicity can.

Transaction introspection is the primitive. A covenant that enforces a trade — checks the amount, the asset, the destination — is the thing that genuinely needs it.

Our design

No keys. Anywhere.

Most covenant swaps still sign something. Mosaik signs nothing — both doors are pure introspection.

  • Four numbers in the tapleaf: asset, amount, maker script, timeout.
  • No maker key. The maker is just an address.
  • The simplest covenant that can possibly enforce a swap.
The demo · centrepiece

We tried to cheat it.

Three fraudulent fills — each a well-formed, balanced transaction the mempool would accept:

  • Underpay the maker  →  rejected
  • Pay the wrong address  →  rejected
  • Hide the maker's output  →  rejected

Watch the lock stop a thief — not just turn for an honest fill.

It's real

Not a mockup. Enforced on-chain.

  • The covenant compiles to a 32-byte CMR and is executed by elementsd with Simplicity consensus rules — the node enforces it, not us.
  • Every fill is one Elements transaction; every settlement and reclaim is on-chain and re-checkable.
  • Multi-asset — L-BTC/asset, asset/asset, both directions.
  • Covered by execution tests and on-chain regtest integration tests — including the rejected attacks.
The stack

Liquid, Simplicity, Nostr, Rust.

Chain
Liquid Network · Elements, confidential, federated
Consensus
Simplicity-capable elementsd · runs the covenant, leaf version 0xbe
Covenant
SimplicityHL · the Tessera — compiled to a CMR (crate tessera)
Settlement
Rust · multi-asset tx construction, SETTLE & REFUND (mosaik-core)
Discovery
Nostr orderbook · offers as addressable events, no server (mosaik-relay)
App
CLI + single-file browser wallet · (mosaik-cli + webapp)
Where it goes

From limit orders to a liquidity layer.

  • Partial fills — sweep a grid of covenant UTXOs in one tx.
  • Recursive vault — a fill re-locks the remainder; fund once, fill many.
  • Cross-L2 legs — a hash-preimage branch bridges to Lightning and RGB.
  • Confidential v2 — covenant settlement over blinded amounts.
